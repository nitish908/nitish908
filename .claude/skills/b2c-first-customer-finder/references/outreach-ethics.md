# Why B2C outreach needs a stricter standard than B2B

B2B first-customer-finding targets a role at a company — someone whose job is to evaluate tools
like yours, and who expects vendor outreach as a normal part of their work. B2C targets a private
person in their own time, often venting about a bad day. Same-looking action (a cold message),
very different power dynamic and expectation. Treat these as hard rules, not style preferences:

1. **Public post ≠ standing invitation.** Someone complaining in a subreddit consented to that
   subreddit reading their post — not to being DMed by a stranger building a product. Prefer a
   public reply on the same thread. If DM is the only channel the platform supports, say so
   explicitly in your reasoning and keep the message unmistakably about their post, not a pitch.

2. **No scraping, no data brokers, no "find their email."** If someone didn't post a contact
   method for the purpose of being contacted, don't go looking for one. This includes reverse
   image search, username cross-referencing to find a "real" identity, or any people-search tool.

3. **One message, not a sequence.** No follow-up cadences, no "just bumping this," no adding
   someone to a list because they replied once. If they don't respond, that's the answer.

4. **Say who you are.** The opener must disclose that you're building/represent the product. Never
   pose as a fellow customer, a neutral commenter, or use a "plant" account to seed hype.

5. **No mass anything.** This skill produces a shortlist of individually-read, individually-scored
   people — not a list to paste into a bulk DM tool. If the user asks for that, say plainly that
   this skill doesn't support it and explain why (platform ToS violations in most cases, spam laws
   in many jurisdictions, and it defeats the "evidence-backed" premise of the skill).

6. **Exclude minors and clearly vulnerable posts.** Skip anyone who appears to be a minor. Skip
   posts that read as a mental-health crisis, financial crisis, grief, or similar vulnerable
   moment even if the surface topic matches your ICP — that's not a sales opportunity.

7. **Geography and legal regimes matter.** Unsolicited commercial messaging to individuals is
   regulated differently by country/platform (e.g. GDPR consent rules in the EU, CAN-SPAM/TCPA in
   the US for email/SMS, platform-specific rules on Reddit/Instagram/etc.). This skill doesn't give
   legal advice — if the user's outreach volume or channel raises this, say so and suggest they
   confirm with counsel rather than assuming it's fine because the post was public.

8. **Honesty about gaps.** If a search comes back thin — few or no real public signals — report
   that plainly. Don't pad the shortlist with weak matches or invented quotes to look more useful.
   A short, honest "here's what's actually out there" report is more valuable than a padded one.
